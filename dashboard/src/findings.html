The Mallory Group

Key Findings

I tested whether supermax contracts hurt performance. They do not. The real story is mean reversion, plus one result I did not expect.

Performance regresses to the mean

Performance regresses to the mean

This is the strongest result in the study. I sorted players into four groups by their impact before signing. The pattern is clean and one-directional.

The bottom group, the players coming off weak seasons, improved by +1.07 on average. Only 24 percent got worse. The top group, the players coming off the best seasons, dropped by -0.80. Seventy-one percent got worse. The two middle groups sat in between, in order.

A regression confirms it. Every point of prior impact predicts a -0.25 drop afterward. The result is about as statistically certain as results get (p below 1e-74, n=2,378).

The supermax penalty is a mirage

The supermax penalty is a mirage

Supermax players post the worst raw decline of any group, -0.63 on average. That looks like proof of a penalty. It is not.

Supermax players are top-group players. They are old, and they sign right after peak seasons. Both facts predict a decline on their own. Once I control for age and prior impact, the supermax effect is +0.48, not negative, and nowhere near significant (p=0.41).

So the contract adds nothing. The decline was already baked in by who these players are.

One caveat. Only 13 players signed supermax deals in this window. That is a small sample. I cannot rule out a small effect that this study is too small to detect. But there is no sign of one, and the estimate points the wrong way for the penalty story.

Changing teams comes with a penalty

Changing teams comes with a penalty

Players who changed teams declined by -0.345 compared to players who stayed. That result is strong and statistically clear (p below 0.001, n=835). It runs against the fresh-start idea, which says a new situation should help.

One caution. Players do not move at random. Teams often let go of players who are already slipping. So part of this gap is probably about who gets moved, not the move itself.

A note on team rankings

A note on team rankings

The team-by-team page ranks franchises by how their signings performed. Read it as description, not a scorecard. Teams with young, rising rosters look good, because their players were due to improve. Franchises built around aging stars look bad, because those stars were due to decline. The ranking reflects roster age and timing more than front-office skill.
